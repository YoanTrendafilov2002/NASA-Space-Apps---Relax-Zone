# NISAR data review

The current explorer ingests NASA ASF NISAR GCOV only. Site coordinates and generalized cartography provide location context. Each raster record identifies the actual granule, frequency, polarization, UTC acquisition, product maturity, source publication, and display extent. The current app catalogue supplies the per-layer evidence and download links.

| Element | Meaning and review |
| --- | --- |
| GCOV values | The real diagonal terms give terrain-corrected gamma-nought power. `HHHH`, `HVHV`, `VVVV`, and `VHVH` correspond to HH, HV, VV, and VH in that order. Frequency A and frequency B availability is specific to each product. A derived dB raster uses `10 log10(power)` for positive power. Its channel record identifies that unit and processing. [ASF product specification and GCOV guide](https://nisar-docs.asf.alaska.edu/gcov/) |
| Display projection | Map PNGs are resampled onto EPSG:3857 for Leaflet, with transparent missing pixels. Image bounds are taken from the projected raster transform and converted back to latitude and longitude. A display stretch gives contrast. Native scientific samples and missing-value tags are carried by the downloadable GeoTIFF. |
| Spatial resolution | A nominal native posting gives spacing between product samples. The effective resolution depends on acquisition and processing. Display regridding can change the apparent map detail. [GCOV pixel spacing](https://nisar-docs.asf.alaska.edu/gcov/) |
| Coverage | Footprints describe the parent acquisition. Published map pixels are limited to a prepared crop and its valid cells. The site area, footprint, and image bounds have different roles. Check the selected raster for actual coverage. |
| Catalogue results | ASF supplies the acquisition metadata and candidate browse assets. The local prepared scenes have downloadable pixels. New records can be inspected before using the browser-based Earthdata subset handoff. [ASF search documentation](https://docs.asf.alaska.edu/api/basics/) · [Harmony workflow](https://nisar-docs.asf.alaska.edu/harmony/) |
| Oil and wetland highlights | Topic colors identify study locations and documented review points. Interpretation of wetness or surface films depends on acquisition geometry, quality, and reference evidence. The visible topic outlines carry no material or event classification. [NISAR Science Users’ Handbook](https://science.nasa.gov/mission/nisar/nisar-handbook/) |

## Questions the assistant can answer

| Ask | What the app checks before answering |
| --- | --- |
| What can I compare at Curuai? | Same native grid, location, polarization and viewing geometry, with measured values on both dates. The August and October HH pair has 562,500 common positive-power cells. The app reports brightness change in those cells. [ASF product guide](https://nisar-docs.asf.alaska.edu/gcov/) |
| Which channel should I use? | The matrix distinguishes measured HH/HV channels from provider listings for every date. HH and HV receive different signal orientations. Their images offer complementary information. [NASA polarimetry guide](https://science.nasa.gov/mission/nisar/polarimetry/) |
| Was this wetland flooded? | Smooth open water often returns little radar energy. Water under plants can produce a stronger return. Confirm event flooding with comparable dates and independent water-level or optical evidence. [L-band flood field study](https://repository.library.noaa.gov/view/noaa/59167) |
| Is that dark patch oil? | Check the patch against surrounding water, wind at the time of the pass and a separate observation or incident record. Calm water and other films can also darken radar. Burgas has one measured HH date in this app. [L-band oil release experiments](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC5856168/) |
| Can this show a drought? | Compare water extent to the usual level for the same season and check rainfall and water levels. The local scene pair measures two dates of radar response. [WMO and GWP drought handbook](https://www.droughtmanagement.info/find/guidelines-tools/handbook-drought-indicators-and-indices/) |

`models/analyze_nisar_matrix.py` reproduces the measurements from the twenty-three native backscatter GeoTIFFs. The question router learns from labelled examples in `data/matrix-assistant-training.json`. `models/train_matrix_assistant.py` fits and evaluates its weights. When installed, local Qwen interprets map commands and ambiguous phrasing. The answer service retrieves the verified measurements and explains the physical processes behind them.

## Reading a change map

The explorer compares georegistered measurements for the same frequency and polarization. It uses cells with positive, usable power on both dates. The Curuai and Marañón change layers mark locations where later radar return rose or fell by at least 3 dB. The threshold describes an approximate doubling or halving of returned power. Export the signed dB GeoTIFF from Source audit to inspect all paired cells at native grid spacing. The GeoTIFF computes `10 log10(later power / earlier power)`, with NaN outside the common usable area. Speckle, vegetation, surface roughness, water and conditions at the acquisition all affect the response. [NOAA SAR Manual, chapter 1](https://www.earthdata.nasa.gov/s3fs-public/2024-06/noaasarmanual_ch01_pg001-024.pdf) · [NASA SAR mathematical tutorial](https://www.earthdata.nasa.gov/s3fs-public/2024-06/sar%20mathematical%20tutorial.pdf)

An Amazon River gauge at Óbidos fell from 606 cm on 13 August to 141 cm on 12 October 2026, relative to its gauge zero. Those daily observations give regional context. The Curuai lake has a separate water level. [August bulletin](https://www.gov.br/ana/pt-br/sala-de-situacao/rio-amazonas/boletins/diario/boletim_01_amazonas_publica-1786634090.62.pdf) · [October bulletin](https://www.gov.br/ana/pt-br/sala-de-situacao/rio-amazonas/boletins/diario/boletim_01_amazonas_publica1310-1.pdf/%40%40download/file)

## Recognition tests on real scenes

| Test | Validation and outcome |
| --- | --- |
| Exposed water at Curuai | The August-fitted HH/HV and local-texture model had 97% water overlap with an independent optical proxy on withheld August areas and 41% in October. It recovered 9% of proxy water-loss cells. The frozen simple HH control had 44% October overlap. |
| Emergent marsh in the Everglades | On one October date, adding HV information raised overlap with the USGS EDEN modeled-depth reference from 66% to 83% in 400 m held-out cells. Quality rules allowed assessment of 2,813 of 4,417 eligible habitat and depth-reference cells. The spatial test does not evaluate transfer to other dates or sites. [USGS EDEN method](https://pubs.usgs.gov/publication/sir20205083) |
| Possible slick near Umm Al Quwain | A coherent-darkening screen fitted off Oman evaluated 172 of 46,942 cells inside a separately mapped reference outline and flagged zero in that group. Most of the outline was unassessable. The public incident record, visual review point and measured screen each have their own evidence. [Incident report](https://www.wam.ae/en/article/c3051mm-oil-spill-from-gem-no-tanker-contained) · [Independent outline](https://www.ess-uae.me/post/satellite-oil-spill-monitoring-uae-umm-al-quwain) |
| Reported spill on the Marañón River | The archived 60 m screen evaluated 246 nearby water cells, found zero connected patches and evaluated zero square metres of the digitized inspection area. The 20 m backscatter change map uses the same two NISAR dates with display coverage. Review the stricter research coverage separately. [OEFA field report](https://www3.congreso.gob.pe/Docs/comisiones2025/transportes/files/oefa_ppt_22_05_2026.pdf#page=10) |
| Measured dark water, Eugene Island and Eloi Bay | An observed-return HH screen fitted on Oman alone compares 60 m means with neighbouring water on both sides. Its fixed 0.65 dB cutoff matched 362 of 1,730 assessed cells in NOAA’s Eugene Island outline, which was mapped 43 minutes later by RADARSAT-2. The outside-outline cells have unknown labels. At Eloi Bay the optical reference was more than five hours later. The site shows its reference boundary only. The new subsets have no pixel-level noise layer for the strict oil test. [NOAA Eugene Island outline](https://www.ospo.noaa.gov/data/ocean/marinepollution/2026/20260827_1210_RADARSAT2_OIL.geojson) · [EMSA radar interpretation](https://emsa.europa.eu/earth-observation-products/how-does-sar-detection-work/items.html) |
| Exposed-water transfer, Atanasovsko Lake | The frozen Curuai threshold required instrument noise at or below −18 dB. At the Bulgarian salt lagoon, HH noise ranged from about −15.15 to −14.79 dB. All radar cells were withheld. The dashed orange outline traces 1,477 water cells in an optical image about 16 hours later. [Ramsar site](https://rsis.ramsar.org/ris/292) |

All scientific radar layers and recognition predictors in this release come from NISAR. Independent optical, field and modeled water references evaluate the research. Source audit records the data and scope for each review outline. The Acquisitions chart uses the median dB across exactly the same usable pixel positions on every listed date. For Qeshm that is three Frequency A HH observations on track 128, frame 15, with the same published processing release and grid. The Harmony subsets omit the noise correction metadata, so the chart describes the published return values.

## Run a new measurement

Use **Acquisitions → Analyze selection** to calculate a change map or a time series for the dates and channel you select. The server verifies the prepared source rasters against their recorded byte counts and SHA-256 values. It requires the same native grid, site, track, frame, pass direction, frequency and processing version and reports any unknown look direction or noise-correction setting. A time series uses the intersection of usable cells across every date you choose and leaves unobserved dates unsampled. Each change run generates a new map and signed dB GeoTIFF. Download the TIFF while the run is available to keep it.

The current exposed-water screen uses both HH and HV and withholds patches that lack enough support from nearby cells. The 60 m screen flags open water in cyan and separately marks possible water edges in dotted tan. At Curuai in October it reduced excess water flags from 10,738 to 4,412 on 33,920 jointly evaluable optical cells. Open-water recall fell from 98.8% to 84.3%. The new Hasik test removed 28 excess flags and missed 64 optical water cells on 8,718 comparable reference cells. The UAE site was withheld because its HV noise exceeded the cutoff. At Alenquer, a spatial transfer test within the same October radar granule reduced excess flags from 1,032 to 200 on 9,095 shared cells. [Wetland comparisons](/science-review-wetlands) · [Alenquer comparison](/science-review-alenquer)

Flooded vegetation requires a different radar signature. The paired research screen requires a matched drier acquisition, valid HH and HV above noise, and a registered vegetation reference. There is no independently labeled unseen matched pair in the bundled cases, so current live runs report the class as withheld. Shark River Slough has a saved review comparing 400 m modeled USGS water depths inside mapped emergent marsh with the separate one-date radar benchmark. The UAE saved review shows earlier optical water within mapped mangrove polygons; the optical image preceded the radar by more than 42 hours. [NISAR Ecosystems ATBD, §3.3](https://gitlab.com/nisar-science-algorithms/ecosystems/documentation/-/raw/main/NISAR_Ecosystems_Combined_ATBD.pdf#page=40) · [USGS EDEN method](https://pubs.usgs.gov/publication/sir20205083)

The oil-review screen reruns the Oman-calibrated observed HH rule at Eugene Island and Eloi Bay. It compares measured return to nearby water on both sides of each assessed cell. Calm water can give a similar signature, and these subsets do not carry the pixel-level noise estimates needed for the stricter oil test. Source audit links each result to its inputs and method. [ASF GCOV guide](https://nisar-docs.asf.alaska.edu/gcov/) · [EMSA oil detection method](https://emsa.europa.eu/earth-observation-products/how-does-sar-detection-work/items.html)
