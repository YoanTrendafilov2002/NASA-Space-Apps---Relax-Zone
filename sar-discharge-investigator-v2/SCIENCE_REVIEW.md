# Scientific evidence review

Reviewed 15 November 2026. The explorer discovers observations and records their provenance. The original investigation screen screens for dark-feature candidates. Identifying a material, mapping flood impacts or establishing drought or source attribution requires validation against independent evidence.

## What each layer supports

| Layer or input | Interpretation and application |
| --- | --- |
| NISAR GCOV L2 | Terrain-corrected γ⁰ linear power on the product grid. The real diagonal layers HHHH, HVHV, VVVV and VHVH represent the HH, HV, VV and VH channel intensities respectively. Acquisition mode and frequency determine availability. An extractor must use the requested diagonal term and report a missing term. The complex off-diagonals and quality mask are separate measurements. Catalogue hits and Harmony job completion do not establish validated local pixels. [ASF GCOV guide and specification](https://nisar-docs.asf.alaska.edu/gcov/) |
| Sentinel-1 RTC | The original browser detector reads the Planetary Computer RTC VV layer through a rescaled 8-bit PNG data endpoint. Its `10 log₁₀(power)` range is −30 to 0 dB, with steps of 30/255 dB. Reaching either endpoint can indicate clipping. Export records this input and the endpoint fraction. The optional Python detector reads floating-point RTC power and uses a separate representation label. Native spacing, independent resolution and analysis-grid spacing differ. [Planetary Computer dataset](https://planetarycomputer.microsoft.com/dataset/sentinel-1-rtc), [Copernicus IW product specification](https://sentiwiki.copernicus.eu/web/s1-products) |
| Browse and basemaps | A catalogue browse image is a display rendering of a parent scene. Where a browse image is placed on a footprint bounding box, registration is approximate. Basemap layers give geographic context and can have other imagery dates. The four-channel matrix comes from per-acquisition metadata and independently validated assets where available. |
| JRC Global Surface Water | The occurrence layer represents historical water detections and has a separate unknown state. The detector screens at 80% occurrence and uses 90% occurrence to describe the share of candidate pixels with frequent water detections. These thresholds are heuristics. The historical map cannot establish shoreline, depth or current inundation for the acquisition date. Missing mapped land distance stays unknown. [JRC occurrence documentation](https://global-surface-water.appspot.com/faq) |
| OPERA DSWx-S1 | A 30 m surface-water extent classification with limited detectability of small and narrow water bodies. Use date, classes, masks and coverage when reporting water extent. Vegetated inundation, wetland identity and drought attribution require other evidence. [NASA PO.DAAC product description](https://podaac.jpl.nasa.gov/dataset/OPERA_L3_DSWX-S1_V1), [JPL specifications](https://www.jpl.nasa.gov/go/opera/products/dswx-product-suite/) |
| Wetland observation timeline | Catalogue metadata establish observation dates, product type, footprint and advertised channels. Scientific points need validated data on comparable grids with known dates, modes and masks. Compare pre-event and event observations or the same season across years with a suitable baseline. Preserve gaps, weather and vegetation effects. Single-date darkening can have multiple causes and flooding under vegetation can brighten radar returns. [NISAR Science Users’ Handbook, wetland chapter](https://nisar.jpl.nasa.gov/documents/26/NISAR_FINAL_9-6-19.pdf), [NISAR calibration and validation plan](https://nisar.jpl.nasa.gov/system/documents/files/27_NISAR_Mission_CalVal_Plan_20180810.pdf) |

## Detector and exports

| Element | Disposition |
| --- | --- |
| Noise screen and radiometry | The Sentinel-1 mission specifies noise-equivalent **sigma** nought (NESZ) of −22 dB or better. For approximate flat water geometry, γ⁰ = σ⁰/cos θ in linear power. In decibels the screen is −22 − 10 log₁₀(cos 39°) ≈ −20.91 dB. The detector retains its rounded −21 dB γ⁰ screening default. This is a nominal approximation, with actual noise, incidence and processing effects unverified. The filter uses background ≥ screen + seed threshold + 1 dB. A feature is flagged low signal if mean γ⁰ ≤ screen + 1 dB or half its filtered pixels lie below that level. A user-supplied GCOV noise screen must be in matching γ⁰ dB. Legacy imported GCOV thresholds with unspecified radiometry remain unverified. [Sentinel-1 mission performance](https://sentiwiki.copernicus.eu/web/s1-mission), [ASF GCOV γ⁰ and γ⁰-to-σ⁰ factor](https://nisar-docs.asf.alaska.edu/gcov/) |
| Selection and background | The original 5×5 Lee-type filter, configurable seed contrast and minimum area remain. Background uses a local estimate followed by a larger window that excludes dark candidates. Requires ≥20 usable background pixels and at least 25% of the analysed-water pixels in that window. Eight-connected segments seed at the selected threshold and grow at half. The minimum region has at least six pixels. The regional size flag uses π times the larger window radius squared, a heuristic for unreliable local contrast. Candidate coverage applies only where water, radar coverage, shoreline, background and noise screens allow the test. A result with zero candidates carries this same limits statement. [Lee filter primary paper](https://doi.org/10.1109/TPAMI.1980.4766994) |
| Area, shape and contrast | Segment area is pixel count × approximate grid pixel area at the window midpoint. Principal axis uses coordinate moments with the corresponding rectangular pixel variances. Extent includes the projected pixel footprint. Contrast is **10 log₁₀(mean background power / mean feature power)**, positive for a darker feature. The browser edge-ring calculation excludes neighbours across row boundaries. The browser distance-to-land map uses a chamfer approximation. Shape and edge contrast describe the segment and cannot identify a film. |
| Uncertainty | The reported sampling term uses an approximate speckle/ENL model assuming independent pixels. Browser terms also include 8-bit quantisation. Python reads floating-point data and omits that display quantisation term. Reprojection need not add independent looks, so the code uses a nominal Sentinel-1 ENL or the NISAR source median number of looks where present. Background estimation, spatial correlation, clipping, resampling, calibration and noise-screen error are outside the reported term. Avoid confidence intervals or cross-platform precision claims based on it. [Copernicus IW spacing, resolution and ENL](https://sentiwiki.copernicus.eu/web/s1-products), [ASF number of looks](https://nisar-docs.asf.alaska.edu/gcov/#number-of-looks) |
| Second acquisition | Tests the same segment coordinates in the common analysis window. Requires a distinct timestamp, a matching reported relative orbit and pass, and at least half of the candidate pixels passing the comparison's eligibility screens. Matching metadata leaves local incidence and calibration differences unmeasured. Below-threshold comparison means lower measured contrast for the eligible pixels on that date. JSON retains the legacy `absent` code for compatibility, with the UI showing “Below recurrence threshold”. Legacy imports without trustworthy comparison metadata display untested. |
| Wind and precipitation | ERA5 is a gridded reanalysis. The app reports the nearest model hour and up to three consecutive hourly precipitation totals. It requires all three for the sum and preserves missing values even at day boundaries. The 0.25° distributed grid cannot resolve sheltering, a plume or a local rain cell. Nominal wind windows from published field and operational studies describe detection conditions. Coarse model winds and missing rain cannot eliminate an explanation. [ECMWF ERA5](https://cds.climate.copernicus.eu/datasets/reanalysis-era5-single-levels), [Open-Meteo Historical Weather API](https://open-meteo.com/en/docs/historical-weather-api), [EMSA wind and detection limits](https://emsa.europa.eu/earth-observation-products/how-does-sar-detection-work/items.html) |
| Physical hypotheses and attribution | Natural and mineral films, differences in wind, currents and other surface conditions can reduce backscatter. Coarse weather and shoreline can provide compatible context. The low-signal flag leaves surface explanations untested. No one of these inputs verifies a pollutant, removes all alternatives or identifies a facility. Bright point targets are unidentified reflectors. An OpenStreetMap match is geographic context and an empty result only describes returned records. Independent identification and source evidence remain necessary. [Surface-wave damping study](https://doi.org/10.1029/JC094iC05p06251), [Multi-frequency field study](https://doi.org/10.1029/97JC01915), [EMSA operational guidance](https://emsa.europa.eu/earth-observation-products/how-does-sar-detection-work/items.html) |

## Historical context and industry matrix

Industry entries are illustrative release pathways and measurement-scope categories. The ordering is editorial. The findings and CSV carry the same categories as the interface.

| Context | Source and limits |
| --- | --- |
| Lake Maracaibo | NASA documents historical oil releases and algal blooms. Those historical optical observations supply context for the hotspot. They do not validate the detector's recent SAR candidates or identify a current facility. Remove infrastructure totals and unsupported quantitative site-attribution claims. [NASA Earth Observatory](https://science.nasa.gov/earth/earth-observatory/troubled-waters-148894/) |
| Houston Ship Channel and Galveston Bay | NOAA documents the 2019 ITC incident and observed pathways. This independently dated event supports historical context. Narrow channels can be excluded by the detector's 100 m mapped-shore buffer. No reference slick raster was matched to the demo acquisitions. [NOAA damage assessment](https://darrp.noaa.gov/node/1668) |
| Bonny River and Ogoniland | UNEP field assessments document historical oil contamination and impacts of artisanal refining in parts of Ogoniland. This is regional context. The geography and age of that evidence do not support a current-source claim for the app's scene. [UNEP field assessment](https://wedocs.unep.org/items/6a723752-f705-41ea-b744-ae2f49f92809) |
| Crude and pipelines, shipping, refining and used oil | Case studies and EMSA document roughness signatures and operational screening for *possible* releases. Weather, geometry, size and sufficient signal matter. A surface roughness signal does not identify a substance, discharge path or legal status. The matrix's “published analogue” refers to mechanism-level precedent. [EMSA screening guidance](https://emsa.europa.eu/earth-observation-products/how-does-sar-detection-work/items.html), [L-band Deepwater Horizon study](https://doi.org/10.1109/TGRS.2012.2185804) |
| Palm oil, food processing, metalworking, pulp and paper, textiles, tanneries | Generic process descriptions and possible pathways. There is no source-linked, site-specific radar validation for these waste streams in this app. A stream could change the surface under particular conditions. The detector has no direct measurement of dissolved concentrations, material composition or suspended load. Composition and review references in the original matrix are background only. |

## Verification and remaining gaps

- The 35 JavaScript synthetic assertions cover dark-feature geometry, speckle-only controls, nominal noise screening, shoreline buffering, temporal contrast, missing orbit and pass information, coarse weather, null precipitation and UTC day boundaries. The original four Python synthetic checks and detector self-test also passed using the installed QGIS Python environment.
- Legacy discovery keeps catalogue metadata separate from raster reads. Its explicit screening action reads 128 × 128 PNG subsets for up to eight recent acquisitions on two concurrent workers. Screening does not change the selected acquisition or prove physical comparability.
- The earlier [validation report](VALIDATION.md) is historical. It describes a reproducibility comparison and synthetic controls, with no field truth or matched analyst slick inventory. Its success rates, old verdict labels and conclusions about site-specific noise or wind do not carry forward to a new acquisition.
- Acquisition timestamp, footprint and channel availability should be distinguishable from measured metrics on the timeline. Inference can suggest and highlight recorded scene IDs. It has no access to a validated oil or wetland classifier. Verified-demo labels apply to the cited saved records and their actual dates.
- Field truth, colocated local weather and shoreline, a per-pixel product noise model, cross-platform calibration, and end-to-end scientific validation of any new detector result remain open. Validation of the full-precision NISAR path on protected data requires an authorized local analysis input.
