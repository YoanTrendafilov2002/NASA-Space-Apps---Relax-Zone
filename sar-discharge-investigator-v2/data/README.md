# Curated explorer data

`explorer.json` supplies six study sites, seven dated Sentinel-1 acquisitions, catalogue footprints, channel availability, and evidence links. The UTC acquisition start comes from the parent ASF GRD record. Where Microsoft Planetary Computer also advertises a matching RTC item, its timestamp and VV/VH asset references are recorded separately. The same Sentinel-1 datatake has a stable `acquisition_id`. Catalogue footprint coverage is the fraction of the study geometry intersecting the provider footprint. Actual raster coverage and calibrated measurements are absent from this snapshot.

| Site | Pinned acquisitions | Map evidence |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Tobago | 21 January, 2 February, 14 February and 26 February 2024. Sentinel-1A IW ascending, relative orbit 164. Matching RTC catalogue records exist for all four dates. | Study rectangle around the documented Gulfstream incident of 7 February. ESA documents the 14 February observation. No slick polygon is supplied. |
| L’Albufera | 19 October, 31 October and 12 November 2024. Sentinel-1A IW ascending, relative orbit 103. The 31 October record is ASF GRD catalogue only. Its exact Planetary Computer RTC lookup returned HTTP 404. | Official RSIS boundary plus a clipped Copernicus EMSR773 AOI01 Delineation v1 flood layer from 30–31 October imagery. |
| Srebarna | Official protected-area geometry and a preset date range for live catalogue discovery. | No curated radar observations or hydrological series. The CLMS evidence link describes the monthly historical product. |
| Lake Maracaibo, Galveston Bay, Bonny River | Existing application study areas for live discovery. | The linked sources describe historical context. Their rectangles indicate the discovery area. |

The original RSIS web service polygons had minor topology errors. The detailed files use GEOS geometry repair and omit zero-area remnants, with relative planar-area changes below 0.0001%. The explorer uses a 15 m topology-preserving display simplification. Source URLs, checksums, validity messages, and attribution are embedded in the files. The Convention on Wetlands makes these RSIS GIS downloads available under CC BY 4.0. [Convention on Wetlands licence information](https://www.ramsar.org/licence-information)

The Albufera vector comes from `observedEventA_v1.json` in the official CEMS AOI01 archive. It retains the flooded-area features that intersect the Ramsar site as individually clipped polygons. The explorer embeds a 20 m simplification of their union. The version, source image acquisition times, and publication credit are retained in the GeoJSON and `explorer-validation.json`. [CEMS activation](https://mapping.emergency.copernicus.eu/activations/EMSR773/) · [CEMS citation guidelines](https://mapping.emergency.copernicus.eu/about/citation-guidelines/)

To regenerate, use Python with `requests`, `Shapely >= 2.1`, and `pyproj`, and run `python scripts/build_explorer_data.py` from the app directory. The script checks the pinned CEMS archive length, retrieves its selected members with byte ranges, and limits the total responses to 16 MB. It reconstructs the output from public metadata and vector data. The resulting `explorer-validation.json` records response sizes and digests. Provider availability or revisions may change, in which case the script stops for review.

The two `basemap-*.geojson` files use public-domain Natural Earth v5.1.2 vectors. They combine global 1:110 million context with simplified 1:10 million detail around the six study sites. Their metadata records exact source URLs, lengths, hashes, and clipping extents. Regenerate them with `python scripts/build_explorer_basemap.py`.
